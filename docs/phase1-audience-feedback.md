# Portfolio Feedback Outreach — Round 1

This is the first real-feedback round for Spencer's portfolio site (https://shrlak.github.io/portfolio/), sent to 2–3 contacts per audience segment: a medtech/biomedical device recruiter, a PI/admissions reviewer, and a peer engineer. For each contact: send the message and task checklist together, then wait for them to actually try the tasks rather than skim and reply with impressions. Log their answers **verbatim** — exact quotes, not paraphrases — and afterward look across contacts within a segment (and across segments) for the same concrete blocker showing up more than once. A single person's one-off opinion is not a signal; the same task failing for two or three different people is.

---

## Segment 1: Medtech/Biomedical Device Recruiter

**Outreach message:**

Hey [Name] — quick favor, should take about 5 minutes. I've been building out my portfolio site (shrlak.github.io/portfolio) ahead of internship/FT recruiting season, and I'd rather find out now if it actually works for someone like you than after I've sent it to 50 companies. Could you open it and go through it as if you'd just received my resume and were deciding whether to move me forward? I've got 5 quick tasks below — no need to write anything formal, just tell me what happened (or didn't) when you tried each one, in your own words. Really appreciate it, and happy to return the favor sometime.

**Task checklist:**

1. Within the first 30 seconds on the site, without searching, state my current degree, school, and grad year, plus one thing you now know I've built or researched.
2. Find the granted patent. Tell me exactly how many clicks/scrolls it took, and what the patent number and status shown were (KR 10-2675388) — could you tell from the page whether it's granted vs. pending?
3. Try to download or access my resume/CV in under 30 seconds. Did you find it? If not, where did you expect it to be?
4. Find a way to contact me (email, LinkedIn, etc.) without using your browser's find-on-page (Cmd/Ctrl+F). How long did it take, and what did you click first?
5. Pick one research project (Pulmonary Assist System or FXII900) and try to state, in one sentence, what problem it solves and what my role/contribution was. Could you answer that from the page alone, or did it feel too vague/technical to summarize quickly?

## Segment 2: PI / Admissions Reviewer

**Outreach message:**

Hi [Name] — quick favor, should take about 5 minutes. I know you review a lot of student research profiles, so I wanted to get your honest read on mine before I start using it for grad school / lab applications. Could you open my site (https://shrlak.github.io/portfolio/) and try the tasks below, then tell me what you actually found (or couldn't find)? I'm not looking for design feedback — I specifically want to know if the research is legible and credible to someone in your position. No need to write anything formal, a quick reply or a few voice notes is perfect. Thanks in advance, I owe you one.

**Task checklist:**

1. Open the Cook Cardiopulmonary Engineering Lab research entry and tell me, in one sentence: what species was used, what was actually measured/tested, and roughly what the sample size or study scale was. If you can't find one of those in under a minute, say which one.
2. Find the patent (KR 10-2675388). Without reading any surrounding description I wrote, tell me just from what's on the page what the device does and what specifically is patented (the mechanism, not just "a cane").
3. There are two conference abstracts listed. Identify which one has actually been presented/accepted (venue + date) versus which is still submitted/pending — is that distinction clear, or did you have to guess?
4. Look at the FXII900 anticoagulation study and try to figure out what my specific role/contribution was (e.g., designed the study, ran assays, analyzed data, just assisted) — can you tell, or is it vague?
5. Pick any one research claim on the site and try to verify it independently — is there a link, DOI, poster file, PI name, or citation attached, or is it just asserted with nothing to check it against?

## Segment 3: Peer Engineer

**Outreach message:**

Hey [Name] — quick favor, should take about 5 minutes. I've been building out my engineering portfolio site and I'd rather get a real read from someone technical than keep guessing on my own. Could you open it (https://shrlak.github.io/portfolio/) and try the tasks below? I'm not looking for design opinions — I want to know if the actual engineering content holds up and reads as credible to a peer. Appreciate it, and happy to return the favor sometime.

**Task checklist:**

1. Pick one research project (Pulmonary Assist System or the FXII900 study) and, within 60 seconds, tell me what my specific technical contribution was — not just "he worked on it," but what I actually did or built.
2. Find the obstacle-sensing cane patent (KR 10-2675388) and see if you can locate any actual technical detail about how the sensing mechanism works. Can you find it, or is it just a patent number with no substance behind it?
3. Pick any one of the 6 engineering projects and try to identify one specific design decision or tradeoff that's explained — not just "designed X." Can you find the reasoning, or is it just a description of the final result?
4. Try to find quantitative results or data from one of the animal studies (ovine or rabbit) — actual numbers/outcomes, not just qualitative claims. Report what you found, or say if you came up empty.
